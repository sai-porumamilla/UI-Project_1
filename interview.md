# Interview notes

Three interviews about the Smart Vinyl Player (UI Project 1). Participants are
anonymized; answers are tidied notes, not verbatim quotes.

## Concepts shown in section 6

Each idea was briefly described or sketched near the end, so it wouldn't bias
the earlier answers:

- A front touchscreen with playback controls and synced lyrics
- A warning not to touch the tonearm while a song is playing
- Physical knobs whose changes show up on the screen
- A phone app with a health score for the player
- A phone app that showcases your collection and listening stats

## Participant 1

### 1.1 Background

**Q1. How do you usually listen to music day to day?**

Through an iPhone or laptop, with headphones.

**Q2. Have you used a record player? Tell me about the last time.**

Hasn't owned one. The cost puts them off, but the aesthetic draws them in, and
it would give them a reason to buy and collect vinyl from favorite artists.

**Q3. Where is or was it set up, and what's around it (speakers, shelves, other
people)?**

Would put it on a side table surrounded by plants, with a shelf underneath
holding a storage box for the records they don't play regularly.

### 1.2 Using the player

**Q4. Walk me through putting on a record, start to finish.**

Takes the record out of its packet and cover, cleans it a little, puts it on the
platter, moves the needle to the desired spot, presses the button to start it
spinning, then plays it from start to finish.

**Q5. What do you do when you want to pause, skip a song or stop?**

Lifts the needle, presses the button to stop the platter, then turns the player
off.

**Q6. Have you ever damaged a record or worried you might? What happened?**

N/A.

**Q7. Which parts of the player feel confusing or fiddly?**

How even the smallest scratch is annoying, especially given how much records
cost and how scratches affect their longevity.

**Q8. How do you know what's playing, or how far into the side you are?**

Knows the order of the songs by heart, or recognizes the pattern.

### 1.3 Controls and feedback

**Q9. When you adjust the volume or switch speeds, how do you know it worked?**

The music sounds faster or slower, or quieter or louder.

**Q10. If the player had a screen, where would you expect it to be, and what
would you want to see at a glance from across the room?**

On the rim of the player, facing forward into the room.

**Q11. Do you ever look up lyrics while listening? How?**

Looks the song up on Spotify or Google.

### 1.4 Care and maintenance

**Q12. How do you take care of the player and your records? How often?**

Cleans a record right after use if needed, and wipes one that hasn't been played
in a long time. Gives the player itself a light wipe-down after each use.

**Q13. How would you know if something was wrong with the player before it
sounded bad?**

If the turntable isn't spinning, or the needle isn't moving.

**Q14. Have you ever had a problem with sound quality (skipping, distortion,
wrong speed)? How did you figure out what caused it?**

Would search Reddit or Google for troubleshooting help.

**Q15. If the player could warn you about a problem, how would you want to be
told: on the player, on your phone, or not at all?**

On the player. They don't want to download a whole other app.

### 1.5 Collection and listening habits

**Q16. Tell me about your collection, or your music library if you don't own
records. Is there anything in it you're especially proud of?**

Proud of their orchestral and classical playlist. It's nice to hear pieces they
have performed in front of audiences of hundreds of people.

**Q17. Do you ever show your collection to other people? How?**

Sends a Spotify playlist, or a song title or link.

**Q18. Do you look at listening stats such as Spotify Wrapped or stats.fm? What
do you find interesting in them, and what's useless?**

Interesting: seeing what kind of music they listened to at different times of
the year. Useless: not much, though the "vibe" profiling is kind of dumb.

**Q19. Would you want stats for records you play on a turntable? What would you
want to see?**

Which songs they skip on a record and which records they play most, organized by
month (for example, the most-played record each month).

### 1.6 Reactions to the concepts

**Reaction. What do you think of these concepts? When would you use them? What
worries you about them?**

Would like to see a small decibel readout below the main display.

### 1.7 Closing

**Q20. If you could change one thing about record players, what would it be?**

Maybe an auto-scanning feature for a record that shows its inner artwork.

**Q21. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?**

Nothing.

## Participant 2

### 2.1 Background

**Q1. How do you usually listen to music day to day?**

Headphones, or through car speakers; usually a chill playlist in the background.

**Q2. Have you used a record player? Tell me about the last time.**

Once. It was interesting to see how it works and a very different experience; it
was cool to see the different ways music can be played and how far it has
advanced. Nothing puts them off, but they have no real reason to use one either.

**Q3. Where is or was it set up, and what's around it (speakers, shelves, other
people)?**

On a shelf in their brother's room, with a built-in speaker system.

### 2.2 Using the player

**Q4. Walk me through putting on a record, start to finish.**

Lifts the needle to make room, places the record so it's secure, pushes the
button to start it spinning, then places the needle on the record to play. Uses
the volume dial and the speed dial.

**Q5. What do you do when you want to pause, skip a song or stop?**

Stopped it by taking the needle off the record. Didn't try skipping songs. An
all-around OK experience.

**Q6. Have you ever damaged a record or worried you might? What happened?**

Not really; they didn't use it for long.

**Q7. Which parts of the player feel confusing or fiddly?**

Knowing where to place the needle to play a specific song.

**Q8. How do you know what's playing, or how far into the side you are?**

It depends on which groove the needle is on.

### 2.3 Controls and feedback

**Q9. When you adjust the volume or switch speeds, how do you know it worked?**

It got louder. It sounds slower if the speed doesn't match the record.

**Q10. If the player had a screen, where would you expect it to be, and what
would you want to see at a glance from across the room?**

A small display showing the minutes and seconds, the song that's playing and how
far into it the record is, with pause, skip and rewind. They would want it on
the bottom half, angled and sticking out.

**Q11. Do you ever look up lyrics while listening? How?**

The lyrics section in Apple Music, synced word for word.

### 2.4 Care and maintenance

**Q12. How do you take care of the player and your records? How often?**

Makes sure there's no dust, that all the parts work, that the record is clean
and smooth, and that the speaker works properly.

**Q13. How would you know if something was wrong with the player before it
sounded bad?**

Looks for visible damage on the player or the disc, or a wonky needle.

**Q14. Have you ever had a problem with sound quality (skipping, distortion,
wrong speed)? How did you figure out what caused it?**

N/A.

**Q15. If the player could warn you about a problem, how would you want to be
told: on the player, on your phone, or not at all?**

On their phone. A screen on the player might not have as much room.

### 2.5 Collection and listening habits

**Q16. Tell me about your collection, or your music library if you don't own
records. Is there anything in it you're especially proud of?**

Doesn't own a record collection, but is proud of a Travis Scott *Astroworld*
record that came from an estate sale with their brother. On their phone, they're
most proud of their personal playlist of deep, carefully curated, heartfelt
songs.

**Q17. Do you ever show your collection to other people? How?**

Shares it with friends through Apple Music.

**Q18. Do you look at listening stats such as Spotify Wrapped or stats.fm? What
do you find interesting in them, and what's useless?**

Yes. Most interesting: top artists by minutes, the number of songs and the
number of artists. Useless: not much; all of it is useful because you can see
who you are in music.

**Q19. Would you want stats for records you play on a turntable? What would you
want to see?**

Related artists, new albums from those artists, the same stats as Apple Music
Replay, the next song on the album while it's playing, and minutes listened.

### 2.6 Reactions to the concepts

**Reaction. What do you think of these concepts? When would you use them? What
worries you about them?**

Would like to be able to share stats and play time with friends.

### 2.7 Closing

**Q20. If you could change one thing about record players, what would it be?**

Being able to share with friends.

**Q21. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?**

Nothing.

## Participant 3

### 3.1 Background

**Q1. How do you usually listen to music day to day?**

On a speaker in their room, on their phone, or through headphones or AirPods
when out. Occasionally uses a record player.

**Q2. Have you used a record player? Tell me about the last time.**

Peaceful and enjoyable.

**Q3. Where is or was it set up, and what's around it (speakers, shelves, other
people)?**

On a shelf, connected to a soundbar on the floor.

### 3.2 Using the player

**Q4. Walk me through putting on a record, start to finish.**

Picks a record from the shelf, removes the outer sleeve, opens the cover and
takes one disc out of its inner sleeve. Places it on the platter and sets a
weight on the center. Their Sony player automatically places the needle on the
outer edge (the beginning) of the record.

**Q5. What do you do when you want to pause, skip a song or stop?**

Presses the up button to stop and the down button to play. To skip, if
desperate, moves the needle past the song, mutes the speaker, or just flips to
the other side.

**Q6. Have you ever damaged a record or worried you might? What happened?**

Never damaged one, but worried they had when a 7-inch record slipped out of
their hand onto the floor.

**Q7. Which parts of the player feel confusing or fiddly?**

None of it.

**Q8. How do you know what's playing, or how far into the side you are?**

Knows it's playing because sound is coming out. Can roughly guess how far in it
is from how far the needle has moved toward the center, but has to open the lid
to see.

### 3.3 Controls and feedback

**Q9. When you adjust the volume or switch speeds, how do you know it worked?**

Volume isn't on the speaker; it's a switch on the back with only a label, which
they rarely use because it faces the wall. A label on the player shows 33 or 45
RPM, and the only feedback is the noise it makes when switched.

**Q10. If the player had a screen, where would you expect it to be, and what
would you want to see at a glance from across the room?**

To the side of the record, toward the front, depending on how big the screen is.
Would expect to see lyrics and how far into the song and album it is.

**Q11. Do you ever look up lyrics while listening? How?**

The lyrics section in Spotify.

### 3.4 Care and maintenance

**Q12. How do you take care of the player and your records? How often?**

Almost never. They don't play records enough to need dusting, and the sleeves
protect them.

**Q13. How would you know if something was wrong with the player before it
sounded bad?**

A crooked needle or visible damage.

**Q14. Have you ever had a problem with sound quality (skipping, distortion,
wrong speed)? How did you figure out what caused it?**

Only skipping, caused by something on the record. Dusting fixed it; if the
problem persisted, they knew it was a scratch.

**Q15. If the player could warn you about a problem, how would you want to be
told: on the player, on your phone, or not at all?**

On their phone.

### 3.5 Collection and listening habits

**Q16. Tell me about your collection, or your music library if you don't own
records. Is there anything in it you're especially proud of?**

A large collection of 78 albums; proud of how many there are.

**Q17. Do you ever show your collection to other people? How?**

Only in person, when friends come over.

**Q18. Do you look at listening stats such as Spotify Wrapped or stats.fm? What
do you find interesting in them, and what's useless?**

Yes, looks at Wrapped. Likes the top 5, top 10 and top 100 songs, and listening
minutes. Can't think of anything useless.

**Q19. Would you want stats for records you play on a turntable? What would you
want to see?**

Yes: number of albums played, total records played, total artists played,
minutes played and their top 10 records.

### 3.6 Reactions to the concepts

**Reaction. What do you think of these concepts? When would you use them? What
worries you about them?**

Pretty good.

### 3.7 Closing

**Q20. If you could change one thing about record players, what would it be?**

Being able to skip by tapping the lyrics.

**Q21. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?**

The player still lets you change speed even when a record is meant for 33 RPM;
there should be a signifier to prevent that.
